How to use this kit

Read in 10 minutes. Use the same day. Every page does one job — copy the prompt, fill the brackets, run it, then verify before you ship.

The teacher-in-the-loop rule for marketing

AI drafts. You decide. Treat every output as a first draft that needs a fact check, a brand pass, and a publish gate. AI cannot tell you what is true about your customers, your numbers, or your market — only you can.

What you will get from this Starter

  • A 4-step content OS — Plan, Create, Check, Improve.
  • A prompt formula — META / INPUT / OUTPUT, the same shape every time.
  • Ten ready prompts covering planning, distribution, conversion, and analysis.
  • A weekly rhythm — 20–30 minutes a day, repeatable.
  • A pre-publish safety pass — five checks before anything ships.

Privacy reminder. Public AI tools (ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini) are not approved data systems. Don’t paste customer PII, internal financials, or anything under NDA. Use general non-identifying context only. See your privacy policy at promptanatomy.space/en/privacy.html.

The framework

Content OS in one page

Plan. Create. Check. Improve. The same loop every week — at any team size.

StepWhat it producesKey questions
1. Plan 30-day plan, weekly themes, one core idea per cycle. Who is the audience? What is the offer? What KPI matters this month?
2. Create One idea expanded into 7 formats; prompts 1–4, 7–9. What channel? What hook? What proof point can we cite?
3. Check Pre-publish safety pass; objections covered; numbers verified. Can we substantiate every claim? Brand-safe? Legally OK?
4. Improve Daily analysis (Action → Decision); 3 prioritized next steps. What underperformed? What is the cheapest test to learn?

One block, every prompt: the marketing context

Before any prompt, fill these five fields once and reuse them. This is the single biggest leverage in the kit.

  • Audience — who, role, pain, market.
  • Offer — what you sell and the outcome it produces.
  • Channels — primary + secondary, with constraints.
  • Goal — KPI and target window (e.g. CPL by Friday).
  • Constraint — brand voice, legal, time, budget.

Save this block in a shared doc. Paste it on top of any prompt as CONTEXT: followed by the prompt body.

Worked example — weekly review

Anonymized B2B SaaS team. Context → Prompt 5 → score → one pre-publish check.

Step 1 — Context block (filled)

CONTEXT:
- Audience: VP Marketing, US B2B SaaS ($8–25M ARR), struggles with CPL rising on LinkedIn
- Offer: Attribution dashboard — cut wasted ad spend by 15% in 90 days
- Channels: LinkedIn ads + email nurture; LinkedIn posts 3×/week max
- Goal: Reduce CPL from $142 to $115 by June 30
- Constraint: No customer names; cite only public case metrics [VERIFY]

Step 2 — Run Prompt 5 (Action → Decision)

Paste context above, then the Prompt 5 mini from page 8 with last week’s CTR, CPL, and conversion by channel.

Step 3 — Expected output (score against this)

  • One underperformer named with $ or % impact (e.g. LinkedIn carousel CPL +28% WoW).
  • Hypothesis separated from facts; one test for next week with owner + deadline.
  • One channel or asset to pause until the test completes.

Step 4 — Pre-publish check

Fail — revise. Output claimed “industry benchmark CPL is $89” with no source. Removed stat; kept directional “CPL above our target.” Pass after edit — ready for internal Slack, not public post.

Core skill

The prompt formula

META / INPUT / OUTPUT. Use it every time. Three fields, one reliable shape.

FieldWhat to include
METAWho the AI is acting as + the goal in one sentence. Example: “You are a US market content strategist. Goal: build a 30-day plan using 4 pillars.”
INPUTConcrete data and bracketed placeholders. Audience, offer, channels, KPIs, target city, budget bands.
OUTPUTExact shape you want — sections, length, table, bullets, tone. Add a verification flag for any unsupported claim.

Bad vs good

Bad

Write a 30-day content plan for our SaaS.

Good

META: You are a US market content strategist. Goal: 30-day plan using 4 pillars (Authority, Problem, Proof, Offer).
INPUT: Audience [VP RevOps, US B2B SaaS], pain [revenue forecast accuracy], USP [closed-loop attribution], channel [LinkedIn + email], target city [Austin].
OUTPUT: 30-day table — topic, hook, format, CTA, KPI, expected outcome. Tone: practical for US B2B.

Prompt safety checklist

  • I named the role (META).
  • I added bracketed inputs the AI can fill.
  • I described the OUTPUT shape (table, bullets, sections).
  • I asked for a verification flag on any unsupported claim.
  • I did not paste customer PII or internal financials.

Prompts 1–5

Plan, create, distribute

1. 30-day content system

When: Start of month. Time: 8 min. Inputs: audience, pain, USP, channel, target city. Output: 30-day calendar table mapped to 4 pillars (Authority, Problem, Proof, Offer).

2. One idea → 7 formats

When: You have one strong idea. Time: 6 min. Inputs: core idea, audience, offer, channel priorities. Output: LinkedIn post, carousel, 30-sec video, email, landing hero, ad copy, 3 hooks.

3. LinkedIn authority post

When: Weekly trust building. Time: 4 min. Inputs: topic, proof point, optional market context. Output: 150–200-word post — hook, 3 points, one concrete example, CTA. Verification flag for every claim.

4. 30-second video script

When: Reels / TikTok / Shorts. Time: 4 min. Inputs: topic, example, audience. Output: Timestamped script — 0–2s hook, 3 beats, proof, CTA, plus one A/B alternate hook.

5. Daily analysis (Action → Decision)

When: Friday wrap-up. Time: 8 min. Inputs: CTR, CAC, CPL, conversion rate, channels, period, goals. Output: Underperforming items, hypotheses, next test (this week), what to pause — with owners and decision deadline.

Tip. Run prompts 1, 2, and 5 weekly. Rotate 3, 4 by content type. The point isn’t volume — it is a repeatable shape your team can ship under pressure.

Prompts 6–10

Convert, prove, and operate

6. Objection handling

When: Sales says “buyers keep saying X.” Time: 6 min. Inputs: top objections, product/offer, audience. Output: 10 pieces — one per objection — with reframing angle, proof, CTA, channel.

7. Lead magnet + DM sequence

When: New campaign launch. Time: 8 min. Inputs: magnet (what it gives + what it solves), audience, offer. Output: 1 lead-magnet post + 4 DMs (opener → qualify → value → offer) with a clear next step in every message.

8. Customer story

When: A customer just got a result. Time: 10 min. Inputs: customer, problem, solution, process, numeric results, optional market. Output: 6 sections — Problem, Solution, Process, Results, Key takeaway, CTA — with a quote slot to verify.

9. Topic cluster (SEO)

When: Quarterly content planning. Time: 10 min. Inputs: pillar topic, niche, offer, audience. Output: 1 pillar outline + 8 subtopics tagged by intent + internal link map.

10. Master prompt (control center)

When: Quarterly reset. Time: 12 min. Inputs: business, audience, pain, USP, revenue goal, channels, data. Output: 6 blocks — 30-day plan, this week’s assets, one idea → 7 formats, 3 test hypotheses, KPI reading guide, 3 actions for tomorrow ranked by priority.

Operating cadence

Weekly rhythm — 20 to 30 minutes a day

A simple cadence beats heroic sprints. Five short blocks a week, repeatable.

DayBlockWhat you run
MonPlanPrompt 10 (control center) → narrow to this week’s 3 actions and the primary KPI.
TueCreate corePrompt 1 update → Prompt 3 (LinkedIn authority) using a fresh proof point.
WedRepurposePrompt 2 (one idea → 7 formats) + Prompt 4 (30-sec video) for the same idea.
ThuConvertPrompt 6 (objection handling) or Prompt 7 (lead magnet + DM sequence).
FriImprovePrompt 5 (Action → Decision) → write next week’s one primary KPI in the channel.

One-hour starter pack (if you only have today)

  1. 15 min — fill the 5-field marketing context block once.
  2. 15 min — run Prompt 1 (30-day system).
  3. 15 min — run Prompt 3 (LinkedIn authority) with one verifiable proof point.
  4. 15 min — run Prompt 5 (Action → Decision) on last week’s numbers.

If a week is on fire, only do Plan + Improve (Mon + Fri). Skip the middle. The loop survives.

Pre-publish safety

Verify before you ship

Print this page. Tape it next to your editor. Run every AI draft through these checks before it leaves your laptop.

The 5-point pre-publish pass

  1. Substantiated. Every number, name, date, and quote has a source you can name. Replace anything you cannot verify with “directionally” or remove it.
  2. On-brand. Voice, claims, and tone match your guidelines. No accidental superlatives (“#1”, “guaranteed”, “best-in-class”) without proof.
  3. Legally clean. No customer PII, no NDA content, no competitive comparisons that imply harm. Disclose endorsements.
  4. Reader-aware. Reads in the audience’s context — role, market, week. Avoids inside-baseball jargon.
  5. Owned. A human (you) approved it. Initials in the doc. Date stamped.

Red flags — revise or discard

  • Confident-sounding statistics or studies you cannot locate.
  • “Customer X grew 5×” without a name, source, and consent.
  • Generic examples with no audience-specific texture.
  • Output that contradicts your privacy or pricing pages.
  • Headlines that promise outcomes you cannot substantiate.

If something is wrong: 1) tell the AI exactly what is wrong and ask for a revision; 2) recheck against the 5 points; 3) if two revisions don’t fix it, write that part yourself.

Plain-English glossary

Shared vocabulary for the whole marketing team — useful when handing prompts to a peer who isn’t in your weekly stand-up.

TermPlain definition
AudienceSpecific role + market + pain (not “SMBs”).
PillarA theme that anchors a month of content (Authority, Problem, Proof, Offer).
USP (unique selling proposition)The one outcome only you produce, in one sentence.
CTA (call to action)The next step you want the reader to take. Verb + outcome.
KPIThe single metric that proves the action worked this period.
HookThe first 2–3 seconds (or one line) that decides whether the post is read or scrolled past.
RepurposeOne idea, many formats — same point, channel-fit phrasing.
Topic clusterOne pillar page + several subtopic pages linked together for SEO.
HallucinationWhen AI invents a fact, source, or quote that looks real but isn’t.
Verification flagA line in the AI output marking what to double-check before publishing.

5-step routine

Use this tomorrow morning

A repeatable routine you can run before your first stand-up — every time you draft with AI.

1
Define — pick the job: plan, create, repurpose, convert, analyze. Write the objective in one sentence.
2
Prompt — paste the marketing context block on top, then the META / INPUT / OUTPUT shape.
3
Verify — run the 5-point pre-publish pass. Solve, look up, edit.
4
Adapt — add your brand voice, examples, and timing. Cut filler that didn’t earn its space.
5
Ship — publish, log the KPI, ask the AI to suggest one tweak for next time.

Tomorrow morning template

Today I will use AI to: [pick one job from prompts 1–10]
The objective is: [one sentence]
Audience and offer: [from the 5-field context block]
Output format: [shape and length]
Quality check: [what I will verify before publishing]

10 takeaways to pin on the wall

  1. AI is a draft assistant, not a strategist.
  2. Better context = better output.
  3. Write the META role, not just the task.
  4. One objective, one primary KPI.
  5. Repurpose before you create from scratch.
  6. Verify every number, name, and date.
  7. Ship the smallest test that reduces uncertainty.
  8. Honest copy beats clever copy in B2B.
  9. Plan + Improve are the loops you cannot skip.
  10. Your judgment is the final check.
